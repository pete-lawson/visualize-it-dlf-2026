Exercise: Build-a-Chart

Same data, your story

Hands-on exercise for the DLF 2026 Forum workshop “Visualize It”: choose a story, then stack layers to build the chart that tells it.

In this exercise you won’t draw a chart from scratch. Instead, you’ll choose a story and then stack ready-made layers in Miro to build the chart that tells it. Every choice is a design decision, and you should be ready to explain why you made it.

There’s more than one right answer, and a few wrong ones.

How it works

  1. Pick a story. Read the data below with your partner and finish this sentence: “The story I want to tell is…”
  2. Build it. In the Miro board, stack one layer for each design decision on your build frame.
  3. Justify it. Add a sticky note next to your chart explaining why you chose each layer.
  4. Review. Swap with another pair and evaluate their chart using the checklist. Find at least one strength and one thing to fix.
TipBuilding tips
  • Choose your measure first. The chart layers are grouped by measure: pick from the dollars drawer or the percent drawer, so your data matches your axis.
  • Chart type and color come as one layer. Find the row for your chart type and the column for your color.
  • Line up each layer. Every layer is 1600 × 1000. Set its width and height to match, and give it the same X/Y position as your build frame.

Questions to discuss

  • Does your measure match your story? When does a percent tell a different story than a dollar amount?
  • Does your chart type fit what you want to show: a part-to-whole, or a change over time?
  • Does every use of color have a reason? Would your chart work for a colorblind reader, or in grayscale?
  • Can a reader understand your chart without looking back and forth to a legend?
  • Can someone state your main takeaway in 5 seconds from the title?
  • Is your chart honest? Think about what “current dollars” means over ten years.
